the intervention.

The thinking behind the programme: how the taxonomy was built, why play works, and what we measure.

Language

Why “toxic” is not enough

Vague words describe a feeling. Precise words describe a behaviour. Behaviour is the only thing anyone can challenge, leave, or report. This is the central argument of the Unmasked method: the gap between “something is wrong” and “this is gaslighting” is the gap between enduring and acting.

See how gaslighting is defined →

Method

Why play works where lecture fails

In a lecture, participants are told what abuse is. In an Unmasked session, they decide publicly and simultaneously with a card in their hand, and then hear the explanation. Getting it wrong in a room where being wrong is safe is what makes the learning stick.

Step inside an Unmasked session →

Discernment

The case for Grey cards

27 of the 100 scenario cards are not abuse. Programmes that only teach recognition create suspicion. Programmes that teach discernment create judgment. Unmasked deliberately includes situations that look like harm and are not, because accuracy protects everyone, including the wrongly accused.

Impact

What we measure, and why we publish it

Baseline survey, post-session forms, end-of-programme survey, facilitator debrief. Four instruments, every programme. They produce the awareness-shift data we report to partners and funders, and the evidence base that keeps the product honest.
