Tactic 03 of 10

Stonewalling

It uses silence as a weapon to make you desperate.

What is stonewalling?

Stonewalling is the deliberate withdrawal of communication, the silent treatment, the refusal to engage, the walking out of a room and refusing to return until you apologise for raising the concern. As a tactic, it is not about needing space. It is about punishment.

The effect is to make the person who raised the concern feel responsible for the silence, and to make them desperate enough to abandon the original issue just to restore connection.

What it sounds like

  • Days of silence after a disagreement, without explanation or timeframe.
  • Leaving the room, the house, or the conversation mid-sentence.
  • Refusing to respond to messages while clearly active on social media.
  • Only resuming communication once the issue has been “dropped.”

What it is not: the Grey card test

Not every withdrawal is stonewalling. The test is: was the withdrawal communicated, with a clear timeframe and a stated intention to return? “I'm too heated right now to be productive, I'm going for a walk, I'll be back in thirty minutes and I want to talk this through properly” is not stonewalling. That is emotional self-regulation in service of the relationship. The difference is communication versus disappearance.

What you can do about it

  • Name it without chasing it.
  • Say: “When you're ready to talk, I'm here. But I won't chase silence.”
  • Set a time limit on waiting, and hold it. Do not apologise for the thing you raised in order to end the silence. That is precisely what the tactic is designed to produce.
