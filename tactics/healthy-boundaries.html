Tactic 10 of 10

Healthy Boundaries & Actions

This is what it looks like when someone respects themselves and you.

What is a healthy boundary?

The tenth category in the Shield is not a tactic of abuse. It is the reference point, the thing we are working toward. Healthy boundaries and actions are the behaviours that demonstrate mutual respect, emotional accountability, and the willingness to work through conflict without weaponising it.

Including this category in the Shield is one of the most important design decisions in Unmasked. Without a reference point for what healthy looks like, communities can only learn to name harm. With it, they can see the contrast, and begin to expect more.

What it sounds like

  • “I hear that you're upset. I'm not going anywhere, can we lower the temperature so we can actually talk about this?”
  • “I'm too heated right now to be productive. I'm going for a walk. I'll be back in thirty minutes and I want to work through this properly.”
  • “I'm so sorry. That wasn't my intention, but I understand why it landed the way it did.”
  • “I don't agree with you, but I hear you. Tell me more about why this matters to you.”

What it is not: the Grey card test

When in doubt, use Sector 10 as the test. Ask: does this feel more like Sector 10, or more like one of the other nine? The answer, honestly arrived at, is the beginning of language.

What you can do about it

  • These ten categories are where Unmasked begins. They are the forms of Normalised Relation Abuse most likely to go unnamed in Nigerian communities today. But they are not the complete picture of how harm operates in relationships, and Unmasked does not claim otherwise.
  • Future editions of the programme will add further tactic categories as the work expands, drawn from community feedback, clinical guidance, and the evolving landscape of relationships across Nigeria and the African continent. The language grows with the communities it serves.
