Tactic 07 of 10

Cultural & Religious Weaponisation

It uses what you love and respect to silence you.

What it is

Cultural and religious weaponisation is what happens when tradition, scripture, cultural expectation, or spiritual authority is invoked not to guide or protect, but to silence a person raising a legitimate concern. The cultural or religious framework is real. The weaponisation of it is the tactic.

In Nigerian communities, this tactic frequently draws on both cultural hierarchy and religious authority, and it is among the most difficult to name, because the framework being invoked is one the person may genuinely love and respect.

What it sounds like

  • “In our culture, a wife does not question her husband like this.”
  • “The Bible says submit. Are you saying the Bible is wrong?”
  • “Your ancestors would be ashamed of how you're behaving.”
  • “This is not how we do things in this family, you're bringing shame.”
  • “The Pastor said women who behave like this are the reason marriages fail.”

What it is not: the Grey card test

The test is: does the cultural or religious framing protect the person or cage them? Does it prioritise their safety or the institution's appearance? Culture and religion can and should be part of healthy relationships. When they are invoked to justify harm or silence legitimate concern, they are being weaponised, regardless of how sincerely they are believed.

What you can do about it

  • Separate the tradition from the weapon.
  • Say: “Culture protects. It does not cage. Faith lifts. It does not trap.”
  • Find a faith leader or community elder who interprets tradition as protection, not punishment. They exist, and they are often more willing to speak than institutions would prefer.
