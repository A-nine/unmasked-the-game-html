The Programme

A structured system
for permanent change.

Unmasked is not a workshop and not a lecture. It is a facilitated behaviour change programme that teaches communities to name Normalised Relation Abuse through play.

How it works

Unmasked is a board game at its surface. Underneath, it is a precision-built behaviour change system with five interlocking components:

  1. 01

    The Scenario Bank

    100 scenario cards across 10 tactic categories, clinically reviewed, drawn from real Nigerian community contexts.

  2. 02

    The Shield

    The game board, with the 10 tactic categories organised around a central shield icon. As cards are played, the board fills. By the end of a session, the taxonomy is visible.

  3. 03

    The Moderator's Manual

    The facilitation protocol that governs every session, including the Opt-Out Protocol, the Grey Card System, the Distress Protocol, and the Closing Ritual.

  4. 04

    The Voting Cards

    Each player holds a Healthy and an Abuse vote card, raised simultaneously so no one can influence another's vote before it is cast.

  5. 05

    The M&E System

    Four monitoring and evaluation forms that capture pre/post awareness shift, session quality, and participant advocacy data for every session.

Together, these five components produce a measurable, replicable, safe behaviour change experience. Every session, every community, every time.

Inside an Unmasked session

  1. 01

    Before the session begins

    The facilitator reads the Opening Protocol aloud to the group. It establishes safety, confidentiality, and the right to opt out at any moment. No one touches a card until every person in the room has heard it.

  2. 02

    The scenario is read

    The active player draws a card. The facilitator reads the scenario aloud to the whole group, a real, recognisable situation from Nigerian community life.

  3. 03

    Everyone votes simultaneously

    All players raise their vote card (Red for abusive, Green for healthy) at the same moment. No one can influence anyone else before the vote is cast.

  4. 04

    The active player names the tactic

    The active player identifies which of the 10 Shield categories the scenario belongs to. If correct, they advance. If wrong, the rest of the group can attempt to steal, and naming the tactic correctly advances them one space instead.

  5. 05

    The explanation is read. Always.

    The facilitator reads the card's explanation aloud regardless of the outcome. This is the most important moment in every turn. The explanation names the tactic, defines it, and explains why this specific scenario fits, or, for Grey cards, why it does not.

  6. 06

    The session closes with a ritual

    Every session ends with the Closing Ritual, a structured moment of acknowledgement, reflection, and resource-sharing. Local support organisations are provided to every participant before they leave.

Not everything that looks like abuse is abuse.

One of the most important features of Unmasked is a set of cards that most awareness programmes never have the courage to include: Grey cards.

Grey cards present scenarios that look like abuse on the surface but, on examination, are not. They teach discernment, not just recognition. A participant who can correctly identify a Grey card, who can say “this is NOT what it looks like” and explain why, has developed genuine awareness. Not paranoia. Not a hair-trigger. Awareness.

27 of the 100 Unmasked scenario cards are Grey. Every session, the discomfort of not being sure is part of the learning.

Every session is measured.

Unmasked runs a four-form monitoring and evaluation system across every programme. Before the first session, participants complete a baseline survey. After each session, they complete a post-session form. At the end of the programme, an end-of-programme survey captures the full knowledge shift. After every session, the facilitator completes a debrief.

This data produces the impact reports that Unmasked provides to programme partners, funders, and advocacy bodies. If you are commissioning an Unmasked programme, impact measurement is built in, not an add-on.
